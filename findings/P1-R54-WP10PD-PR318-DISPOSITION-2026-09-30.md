## Ruling (a): the empty-family trace satisfies the oracle as published

Yes. Published `model.transaction("DUP-EMPTY-BLANK")` specifies, per tapefs §9.5 step 3 → §9.6 step 3:

```
w A0h(8) w A1h(136) w B0h(264) w B1h(392) f | w A0h(8) w B0h(264) f | w M(6144) f | w P(0) f
```

That is one flush after B0's header and none between A0 and B0. The oracle binds the empty-family
case's `trace_sha256` to that full transaction. The observed trace `8c17fab7…f5e4` equals it. The
observed family is promote `INVALID_ARG`, re-spool `OK`, arm `OK`, zero-frame commit `OK`, each with
empty `block_events` (reads included). Side-B mount is `TAPE_OK`. All of this matches the published
expectation.

Step 6 shows the oracle discriminates: the pre-fix `A0h, f, B0h, f` order fails and the fixed order
passes. The oracle was not changed; the published assertion predates the fix. PM's §9.5 → §9.6 reading
(#305 pass 3) is the same reading the oracle had already encoded.

Observation: the pre-fix defect was caught **only** by trace binding. No remount outcome differed,
because the extra flush is a durability-order deviation without an outcome consequence at these
boundaries. Trace binding is therefore load-bearing for row 1 and must stay mandatory.

### V-R54-01 (minor, process): the gate's anchor is writable in the same PR

in `tests/IMPORTS.json` (whose `evidence_bundles` lists only the two `docs/verification/runs/` bundles)

changed.

`run_product.py`. My disposition records anchor the accepted hashes independently, and I confirmed
the four bundles at this head against them.
